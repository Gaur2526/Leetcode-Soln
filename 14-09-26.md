# 836. Rectangle Overlap

**Date Solved:** September 14, 2026

**Status:** Solved

## Problem Statement

An axis-aligned rectangle is represented as a list `[x1, y1, x2, y2]`, where `(x1, y1)` is the coordinate of the bottom-left corner, and `(x2, y2)` is the coordinate of the top-right corner. Its top and bottom edges are parallel to the X-axis, and its left and right edges are parallel to the Y-axis.

Two rectangles overlap if the area of their intersection is **positive**. To be clear, two rectangles that only touch at the corner or edges do not overlap.

Given two axis-aligned rectangles `rec1` and `rec2`, return `true` if they overlap, otherwise return `false`.

---

## Examples

### Example 1

Input:

`rec1 = [0,0,2,2], rec2 = [1,1,3,3]`

Output:

`true`

The two rectangles overlap in the region from `(1,1)` to `(2,2)`. The intersection has positive width and positive height.

### Example 2

Input:

`rec1 = [0,0,1,1], rec2 = [1,0,2,1]`

Output:

`false`

The rectangles only touch along the edge `x = 1`, so the intersection has zero area.

### Example 3

Input:

`rec1 = [0,0,1,1], rec2 = [2,2,3,3]`

Output:

`false`

The rectangles are completely separated from each other.

---

## Constraints

* `rec1.length == 4`
* `rec2.length == 4`
* `-10^9 <= rec1[i], rec2[i] <= 10^9`
* `rec1` and `rec2` represent a valid rectangle with a non-zero area.

---

# Approach

Two axis-aligned rectangles overlap with **positive area** only if they overlap on both the X-axis and the Y-axis.

Instead of calculating the complete intersection area, we can independently check whether the rectangles have a positive overlap in both dimensions.

For:

`rec1 = [x1, y1, x2, y2]`

and:

`rec2 = [a1, b1, a2, b2]`

we check:

`Positive X overlap && Positive Y overlap`

If both are true, the rectangles overlap.

---

## Step 1: Check X-Axis Overlap

The horizontal range of `rec1` is:

`[x1, x2]`

The horizontal range of `rec2` is:

`[a1, a2]`

The left boundary of their intersection is:

`max(x1, a1)`

The right boundary of their intersection is:

`min(x2, a2)`

Therefore, there is a positive horizontal overlap if:

`min(x2, a2) > max(x1, a1)`

The strict `>` comparison is important.

If both values are equal, the rectangles only touch along an edge and the overlap width is `0`.

---

## Step 2: Check Y-Axis Overlap

Similarly, the vertical range of `rec1` is:

`[y1, y2]`

The vertical range of `rec2` is:

`[b1, b2]`

The bottom boundary of their intersection is:

`max(y1, b1)`

The top boundary of their intersection is:

`min(y2, b2)`

Therefore, there is a positive vertical overlap if:

`min(y2, b2) > max(y1, b1)`

Again, we use a strict `>` comparison because the intersection must have positive height.

---

## Step 3: Combine Both Conditions

For the intersection area to be positive, both the width and height must be positive.

Therefore:

`min(x2, a2) > max(x1, a1) && min(y2, b2) > max(y1, b1)`

If both conditions are true, the rectangles overlap.

Otherwise, they do not.

---

# Complexity

There are only a constant number of comparisons and arithmetic operations.

Therefore:

`Time: O(1)`

`Space: O(1)`

---

# Solution

```cpp
class Solution {
public:
    bool isRectangleOverlap(vector<int>& rec1, vector<int>& rec2) {
        return min(rec1[2], rec2[2]) > max(rec1[0], rec2[0]) &&
               min(rec1[3], rec2[3]) > max(rec1[1], rec2[1]);
    }
};
